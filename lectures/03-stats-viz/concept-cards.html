Aggregate collapses each pile to one row. A window function keeps every row in place and fills in a value computed from its neighbour inside the same pile.
Row 0 and row 3 are NaN: each is the first scan of its task, so there is nothing above it to subtract. That is the group boundary doing its job. Drop the groupby and pandas will subtract task A's last scan from task B's first one — inventing a gap that never happened.

Same shape, other window functions: .shift() (peek at the previous row), .cumsum() (running total), .rank() (position inside the group), .rolling(n).mean() (moving average). In SQL they are written OVER (PARTITION BY task_id ORDER BY event_time) — same idea, different spelling.

What a boxplot is made of

Nine delivery delays, sorted: 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 14, 30. The box is drawn from the same quantiles you can compute by hand.

The red dot is not an error. It is one real observation sitting far from the middle — in our warehouse, those dots are the 44-minute pauses, the most interesting rows we have.
The box says nothing about how many points there are. A box built from 12 tasks looks exactly like a box built from 12,000. Comparing groups of different sizes? Say the sizes out loud.

Two groups? Draw histograms instead — they show the shape. Five or more groups? Boxes, because histograms stop being readable.
